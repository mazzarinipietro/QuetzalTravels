CULTURE

A Culture Built on Two Oceans

Panama is a crossroads of civilizations, where indigenous Kuna wisdom, the legacy of the great Canal, and the colonial grandeur of Casco Viejo converge into a singular cultural identity.

From mola textiles crafted on the San Blas islands to the cosmopolitan pulse of Panama City, this is a nation of bridges — between oceans, cultures, and centuries.

Kuna Heritage

The Kuna people of San Blas preserve ancient traditions through vivid mola textiles and deep ocean knowledge.

Canal Legacy

The Panama Canal redefined global trade and remains a living symbol of human ambition and engineering triumph.

Colonial Casco

Cobblestone streets and faded colonial facades hide vibrant art galleries, jazz bars, and rooftop views of the skyline.

FESTIVALS

Celebrations That Unite Two Worlds

Panama's festivals are a joyful testament to the richness of its multicultural soul, drawing together indigenous, Spanish, and Caribbean traditions.

From the thunderous rhythms of Carnival to the solemnity of ancestral remembrance, each celebration reveals another layer of Panama's layered identity.

Carnival

Four days of music, water battles, and elaborate costumes that bring the whole nation to the streets in joyful chaos.

Festival de las Mil Polleras

The largest pollera festival in the world, held every January in Las Tablas, where thousands of women proudly wear the iconic hand-embroidered pollera dress, celebrating Panama's most cherished national symbol through music, dance, and color.

Corrida de Toros Panameña

A raw and festive tradition rooted in Panama's rural culture, where local riders and brave volunteers face bulls in open fields — no fancy matadors, just pure countryside spirit and community celebration.

CUISINE

Flavors Where Two Oceans Meet

Panamanian cuisine blends indigenous roots, Spanish colonial flavors, and Afro-Caribbean influences into a rich culinary tradition shaped by two coastlines.

From hearty mountain stews to light Pacific ceviches, every dish tells the story of a country that stands between two great oceans and draws the best from both.

Sancocho

Panama's national dish — a soul-warming chicken stew with yuca, plantain, and fragrant culantro.

Ropa Vieja

Tender shredded beef in a rich tomato sauce, slow-cooked until it melts into a deeply satisfying comfort dish.

Carimañolas

Traditional Street Food & Home Cooking A beloved Panamanian comfort food made from yuca dough stuffed with seasoned ground meat or cheese, then fried to a golden crisp. A staple at family tables and street stalls across the country, especially popular at breakfast.

Patacones

Twice-fried green plantain slices, crispy and golden, the beloved snack of Panamanian street food culture.

Hojaldres

Soft, airy fried bread served at breakfast alongside eggs and cheese — a simple Panamanian morning classic.

Chicheme

A creamy, sweet corn drink flavored with cinnamon and vanilla, a beloved refreshment across all of Panama.

NATURE

Wildlife at the Crossroads of the Americas

Panama sits at one of Earth's great biological crossroads, where North and South American species intermingle in some of the planet's most biodiverse ecosystems.

From the cloud forests of Chiriquí to the coral gardens of Bocas del Toro, Panama's wildlife is as extraordinary as its geography.

Harpy Eagle

Jungle Canopy

One of the world's largest and most powerful eagles, Panama's national bird commands the forest canopy.

Golden Frog

Rainforest Floor

Panama's most iconic amphibian, a vibrant golden symbol of good luck now critically endangered in the wild.

Brown Pelican

Tropical Forests & Garden Highlands

A jewel of Panama's highlands, this tiny but fierce hummingbird hovers with impossible precision over flowering plants, its emerald tail catching the light like a living gemstone. One of the country's most enchanting and fast-moving wildlife sights.
