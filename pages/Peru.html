CULTURE

A Culture Carved into the Mountains

Peru is the heart of the ancient Inca world, a civilization that built cities in the sky and wove entire cosmologies into stone, cloth, and ceremony — traditions that remain alive in the highlands today.

From the Sacred Valley to the depths of the Amazon, Peruvian culture is a profound encounter with a living heritage that spans thousands of years and dozens of distinct indigenous communities.

Inca Heritage

Temples, terraces, and mountain citadels reveal the genius of an empire that ruled the Andes for centuries.

Textile Traditions

Hand-woven alpaca textiles carry ancestral symbols and stories in every thread, a living art form of the Andes.

Machu Picchu

The lost city of the Incas, perched 2,430 metres above sea level — one of humanity's most breathtaking achievements, hidden in the clouds.

FESTIVALS

Celebrations as Ancient as the Andes

Peru's festivals are rooted in the rhythms of the mountains and the cosmos, blending Andean spirituality with Catholic devotion in celebrations of rare power and beauty.

From the Festival of the Sun at Cusco to the flower-carpeted processions of Corpus Christi, each event is a bridge between the ancient and the living.

Inti Raymi

The Festival of the Sun draws thousands to Cusco each June for a grand Inca ceremony honoring Inti, the sun god.

Señor de los Milagros

Peru's most sacred procession, where thousands dressed in purple fill Lima's streets every October to honor the iconic colonial-era mural.

Corpus Christi

Saints are carried through Cusco's streets in solemn processions that blend Catholic faith with Andean spiritual roots.

CUISINE

Flavors Discovered at the Top of the World

Peruvian cuisine has taken the world by storm, and rightly so — it draws from the extraordinary biodiversity of three distinct ecosystems: the Andes, the Amazon, and the Pacific coast.

From the acid brightness of ceviche to the deep, smoky warmth of Andean stews, every dish in Peru is a journey through altitude, history, and flavor.

Anticuchos

Tender beef heart skewers marinated in spices and grilled over open flame, a beloved Peruvian street food classic.

Lomo Saltado

A stir-fry of beef, tomatoes, and fries in soy and vinegar — where Peruvian and Chinese culinary traditions meet.

Aji de Gallina

Shredded chicken in a rich, golden aji amarillo cream sauce — comfort food with a distinctly Peruvian warmth.

Causa Limena

Layers of yellow potato mashed with lime and aji, filled with tuna or chicken — elegant, cold, and unmistakably Limeñan.

Pisco Sour

Peru's iconic cocktail — pisco grape brandy shaken with lime juice, egg white, and bitters, crowned with foam.

Picarones

Sweet pumpkin and sweet potato fritters drizzled in chancaca syrup — a beloved Peruvian street food dessert.

NATURE

Wildlife Between the Andes and the Amazon

Peru is one of the most biodiverse countries on Earth, encompassing the world's deepest canyon, the mightiest river, and high-altitude ecosystems found nowhere else on the planet.

From the soaring condors of the Colca Canyon to the pink river dolphins of the Amazon basin, Peru's wildlife reflects the extraordinary range of its landscapes.

Andean Condor

Colca Canyon

With the largest wingspan of any land bird, the condor soars above the Colca Canyon as a sacred symbol of the Andes.

Llama

Andean Highlands

Graceful and indispensable to Andean life for millennia, llamas still roam the highlands as a living symbol of Peru.

Pink River Dolphin

Amazon River

The mythical boto of the Amazon, these rare pink dolphins glide through the jungle rivers of the Peruvian basin.

Spectacled Bear

Cloud Forest

South America's only bear, this elusive creature roams the misty cloud forests of the Andean slopes.
